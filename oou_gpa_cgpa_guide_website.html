01 · The foundations

What are GPA and CGPA?

They both describe academic performance, but they cover different periods of your university journey.

One semester
GPA — Grade Point Average

GPA measures your academic performance in a particular semester, taking the credit units of your courses into account.

Across semesters
CGPA — Cumulative Grade Point Average

CGPA measures your overall academic performance across all completed semesters.

Think of it this way: Your first-semester GPA tells you how you performed in that semester. After another semester, your CGPA reflects your performance across both semesters, weighted by credit units.

Credit units

The numerical weight assigned to a course.

Grade point

The numerical value assigned to the grade earned.

Quality points

Credit units multiplied by the course grade point.

03 · Semester calculation

How to calculate your GPA

You need each course's credit units, the grade point you earned, and the total credit units for the semester.

GPA = Total Quality Points ÷ Total Credit UnitsQuality Points = Course Credit Units × Grade Point
1

Multiply each course's credit units by its grade point.

For example, 3 credit units × 5 grade points = 15 quality points.

2

Add the quality points from all courses.

For example: 15 + 12 + 10 + 8 + 3 = 48.

3

Add all course credit units.

For example: 3 + 3 + 2 + 2 + 1 = 11.

4

Divide total quality points by total credit units.

48 ÷ 11 = 4.36.

05 · Cumulative calculation

How to calculate your CGPA

CGPA combines the quality points and credit units from all completed semesters.

CGPA = Total Quality Points ÷ Total Credit UnitsWhen using semester GPAs: CGPA = Σ (Semester GPA × Semester Credit Units) ÷ Σ Semester Credit Units

Worked example

Total quality points = 48 + 60 = 108. Total credit units = 11 + 14 = 25.

CGPA = 108 ÷ 25 = 4.32

A common mistake: Do not automatically add semester GPAs and divide by the number of semesters. That simple average can be wrong when semesters have different total credit units. Always use the weighted calculation.

Another weighted CGPA example

Semester 1: GPA 4.00 with 20 units. Semester 2: GPA 5.00 with 10 units.

(4.00 × 20) + (5.00 × 10) = 130
130 ÷ (20 + 10) = 4.33 / 5.00

The semesters do not have equal weight because they have different credit units.

06 · Building good habits

Your academic journey is not over

A low GPA does not automatically mean your academic journey is over. Your CGPA can improve when you perform better in subsequent semesters, although the extent depends on previous results and the credit units you take.

01

Know your credit units

Understand which courses carry more weight.

02

Attend lectures consistently

Avoid creating unnecessary gaps in your understanding.

03

Study throughout the semester

Do not rely entirely on last-minute preparation.

04

Practise relevant past questions

Use them to identify areas that need improvement.

05

Track your results

Calculate your GPA after every semester and monitor your progress.

06

Seek help early

Ask questions when you struggle with a topic.

07

Set realistic academic goals

Review your progress regularly and adjust your study habits when necessary.

Your GPA is a measure of academic performance. Use it as information that helps you make better decisions, not as the only measure of your worth or potential.
07 · Quick answers

Frequently asked questions

Why do credit units matter?

Credit units are the weight assigned to each course. A course with more units contributes more to total quality points and therefore has more influence on GPA.

What is a quality point?

It is the course's credit units multiplied by the grade point earned. For example, 3 units with an A worth 5 points gives 15 quality points.

Can my CGPA improve next semester?

Yes. Better performance in later semesters can improve your cumulative result. The amount of change depends on your previous results and the credit units in each semester.

Is the GPA calculator an official OOU result system?

No. It is a personal learning and estimation tool based on the grading scale and course units in the materials supplied. Use your official OOU result and programme rules for final confirmation.
